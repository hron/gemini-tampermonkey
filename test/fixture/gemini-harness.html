Prompt 1: Explain orbital mechanics briefly

Orbital mechanics describes the motion of spacecraft and celestial bodies under gravity.

Kepler's first law states that every planet's orbit is an ellipse with the Sun at one focus.

Kepler's second law states that a line segment joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time.

Kepler's third law relates the square of the orbital period to the cube of the semi-major axis.

Hohmann transfer orbits provide a fuel-efficient two-impulse maneuver between two coplanar circular orbits.

Escape velocity depends on the mass of the central body and the radial distance from its center.

Specific orbital energy remains constant along an unperturbed Keplerian trajectory.

Lagrange points are five positions where gravitational and centripetal forces balance in a two-body system.
